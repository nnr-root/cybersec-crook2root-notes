# 🐧 Linux

> [!abstract] Sub-index of [[OS Internals]]
> A rigorous curriculum from first shell to kernel-level reasoning: platform selection, command-line fluency, boot, storage, processes, networking, hardening, observability, authorized privilege-boundary analysis, and kernel internals.

## 🗺️ Zero-to-Mastery Learning Path

1. [[Linux Introduction and Distributions|Linux Introduction & Distributions]] — identify the kernel/userland boundary, choose a distribution, and justify native, virtual, container, or remote deployment.
2. [[Linux CLI and Core Commands|Linux CLI & Core Commands]] — build complete command-line orientation and a dependable core-command vocabulary.
3. [[Linux IO Redirection and Piping|Linux I/O Redirection & Piping]] — compose processes safely through descriptors, pipelines, substitutions, and exit-status control.
4. [[Linux File System Hierarchy|Linux File System Hierarchy & Editors]] — master FHS, VFS path resolution, inodes, mounts, metadata, pseudo-filesystems, and editors.
5. [[Linux Boot Process & systemd]] — follow firmware through initramfs and PID 1, then operate and troubleshoot systemd units.
6. [[Linux Permissions and Processes|Linux Permissions & Process Management]] — reason about identities, modes, ACLs, signals, jobs, process lineage, and supervised services.
7. [[Linux Memory & Storage Internals]] — connect virtual memory, allocators, page cache, filesystems, LVM, dm-crypt, and LUKS.
8. [[Linux Networking and Transfers|Linux Networking, Transfers & Curl]] — trace sockets through routes, DNS, netfilter/nftables, namespaces, controlled transfers, and HTTP.
9. [[Linux Security Controls & Hardening]] — layer capabilities, seccomp, LSM policy, PAM, service sandboxes, and exploit mitigations.
10. [[Linux Observability Logging & Forensics|Linux Observability, Logging & Forensics]] — correlate journal, audit, syscall, profiling, eBPF, disk, memory, and crash evidence.
11. [[Linux Privilege Escalation|Linux Advanced Mechanics & Privilege Escalation]] — analyze authorized PATH, schedule, SUID/SGID, capability, sudo, and identity-transition failures.
12. [[Linux Kernel Internals]] — master syscall ABI, scheduling, namespaces, cgroups v2, eBPF, modules, io_uring, and crash debugging.
13. [[Linux Documentation and Note-Taking|Linux Documentation & Note-Taking]] — turn technical work into scoped evidence, reproducible findings, timelines, and durable knowledge.

## Progression standard

- **Crook:** explain vocabulary and reproduce the bounded lab exactly.
- **Operator:** diagnose an unfamiliar Linux host from first principles and preserve evidence of every conclusion.
- **Root:** reason across firmware, kernel, userspace, policy, storage, networking, and observability; design controls and recovery rather than memorizing commands.
